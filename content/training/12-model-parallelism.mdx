Data parallelism gives every device the whole model. When the model is too big for one device, it has to be cut up: across devices layer by layer (a pipeline), inside each layer (tensor parallelism), along the sequence, or among experts. Below, a batch flows through a four-device pipeline in micro-batches, every pass simulated as an event; the empty space is the _bubble_, time the devices spend waiting.

**Pipeline parallelism.** Give each of $p$ devices a contiguous block of
layers, chapter 4's stack cut into stages: device 1 holds the first quarter of
the layers, device 4 the last. A sequence's activations flow forward from
device to device and its gradients flow back. One batch at a time would leave
three devices idle at any moment, so the batch is cut into $m$
_micro-batches_ that follow each other through the stages. GPipe ([Huang et al.
2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.06965)) runs every micro-batch forward, then
every one backward; at the start and end of each step the pipeline fills and
drains, and those idle slots are the bubble, a fraction $(p - 1)/m$ of the
useful work: <R k="pipeline.rows.0.bubble" pct d={1} /> for 4 devices and 8
micro-batches in the simulation (the formula gives the same, to the last bit;
results.md §12). _1F1B_ (one forward, one backward) interleaves each device's
forwards and backwards once the pipeline is full: the bubble is the same, but a
device holds at most $p$ micro-batches' activations instead of all $m$
([Narayanan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.04473), §2.2). Megatron-LM's
_interleaved_ schedule gives each device $v$ smaller chunks of layers spread
round the pipeline (device 1 holds layers 1–2 and 9–10, say), cutting the
bubble to $(p - 1)/(v\,m)$: <R k="pipeline.rows.2.bubble" pct d={1} /> with
$v = 2$, <R k="pipeline.rows.3.bubble" pct d={1} /> with $v = 4$, at the price
of $v$ times as many sends between devices. Pick a schedule above and watch
where the idle time goes.

**Tensor parallelism.** Cut each layer's matrices instead
([Shoeybi et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08053), Megatron-LM). The
feed-forward network's first matrix ([Part 1, chapter 4](/learn/04-ffn)) is
split by columns, so each of $t$ devices computes its own slice of the hidden
layer, GELU included, with no communication; the second matrix is split by
rows, so each device produces a partial sum of the output, and one all-reduce
adds them. Attention splits the same way, a group of heads per device
([Part 1, chapter 3](/learn/03-attention): the heads are already independent).
That is two all-reduces of the activations per layer in the forward pass and
two in the backward pass, every layer, every micro-batch: far more
communication than data parallelism's once-per-step gradient sync, which is
why tensor parallelism stays inside one server, on NVLink. In the systems
model, Megatron's 18.4-billion-parameter run ($t = 8$, no pipeline) spends <R k="systems.validation.rows.3.breakdown.tensor" d={2} /> s of its <R k="systems.validation.rows.3.breakdown.time" d={2} /> s step in those
all-reduces.

**Sequence and context parallelism.** Tensor parallelism shares the matrix
products but not LayerNorm or dropout, whose activations each device would
still hold in full. _Sequence parallelism_ ([Korthikanti et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05198))
splits those along the sequence, turning each all-reduce into a reduce-scatter
and an all-gather (the same bytes) and cutting the activations per device by
$t$. For very long sequences, _context parallelism_ splits the sequence itself
across devices for attention too: each device keeps its own queries and gets
everyone's keys and values, passed round a ring while computing
([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01889), ring attention) or
all-gathered first, as Llama 3 does for its 128K-token stage
([Grattafiori et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21783), §3.3.2).

**Expert parallelism.** A mixture-of-experts model replaces each feed-forward
network with many experts, of which each token uses one or two
([Lepikhin et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.16668), GShard;
[Fedus et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.03961), Switch Transformers).
The experts live on different devices; every layer sends each token to its
expert's device and back with two _all-to-all_ exchanges, where every device
sends a different piece to every other.

**3-D and 4-D layouts.** Real runs combine them: tensor parallelism inside a
server, pipeline stages across servers, data parallelism (often sharded, ZeRO
or FSDP, chapter 11) outermost. Llama 3 405B trained with four dimensions,
[tensor, context, pipeline, data], on up to 16,384 GPUs; its first stage used
8-way tensor, 16-stage pipeline and 64-way data parallelism (Table 4). Choosing
a layout is a trade between the memory each device must hold and the time lost
to communication and bubbles. The explorer below runs this site's systems model
on three published model shapes: try fitting GPT-3 into 80 GB, then see what
the bubble costs with few micro-batches.

<Layer kind="maths">

**Tensor parallelism's communication**, per layer per micro-batch of $b$
sequences of length $s$ and width $h$ over $t$ devices: 4 all-reduces of
$2\,s\,b\,h$ bytes (6 with full recomputation), each costing

$$
t_{\text{AR}} = 2(t-1)\,\alpha + 2\,\tfrac{t-1}{t}\,\tfrac{2sbh}{B_{\text{NVLink}}}
$$

**Pipeline**, $p$ stages, $m$ micro-batches, $v$ chunks per device, a
micro-batch's forward and backward on one stage taking $t_f + t_b$:

$$
T = \Big(m + \tfrac{p-1}{v}\Big)(t_f + t_b), \qquad \text{bubble} = \frac{p - 1}{v\,m}
$$

**Activations on the first stage** (Korthikanti et al., Table 2 and §4.2.3),
bytes per layer with tensor and sequence parallelism, times $L$ layers' worth
(the first stage holds $p$ micro-batches of its $L/p$ layers) and
$1 + \frac{p-1}{p\,v}$ when interleaved:

$$
\frac{s\,b\,h}{t}\Big(34 + \frac{5\,a\,s}{h}\Big)
$$

**Step time** in the systems model: $T = (m + \tfrac{p-1}{v})\,(c + \tau + \kappa) + T_{\text{p2p}} + T_{\text{DP}}$,
with $c$ the compute of one micro-batch on one stage at $\eta$ of peak, $\tau$
its tensor-parallel all-reduces, $\kappa$ its context-parallel all-gathers,
and nothing overlapped.

if (kind === "1f1b") {
  const warm = Math.min(p - s - 1, m);
  for (let i = 0; i < warm; i++) out.push(["F", i, 0]);
  let f = warm;
  let b = 0;
  while (f < m) {
    out.push(["F", f++, 0]);
    out.push(["B", b++, 0]);
  }
  for (let i = b; i < m; i++) out.push(["B", i, 0]);
  return out;
}
