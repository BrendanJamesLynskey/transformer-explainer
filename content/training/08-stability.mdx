Large training runs fail in two ways that small ones rarely show: the loss suddenly jumps (a _spike_) and either recovers or never comes back, or it quietly stops improving because part of the model has saturated. Both can be reproduced on the Decoder's model. Below, the model trains at ten times the default learning rate, and at a few saved steps the site runs its own forward pass on the saved weights: watch one attention head's pattern collapse onto single characters as its scores grow by orders of magnitude.

**Attention-logit growth.** At a high learning rate, the query and key weights grow, the scores $q^\top k/\sqrt{d_k}$ grow with their product, and the softmax turns into a hard maximum: each position attends to exactly one other, the attention entropy falls towards zero, and the gradient through the softmax vanishes, so attention stops learning ([Decoder, chapter 3](/learn/03-attention) shows the healthy version). [Dehghani et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.05442) saw exactly this diverge an 8-billion-parameter vision transformer; [Wortsman et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14322) showed the same instability appears in small models at high learning rates. Here, at a peak learning rate of 0.1 (warmup, cosine, clip 1.0, three seeds), the largest attention score reaches <R k="attnGrowth.rows.3.meanStats.-1.maxScore" d={0} sep /> on average and the mean attention entropy falls to <R k="attnGrowth.rows.3.meanStats.-1.attnEntropy" d={2} /> nats (at the default 0.01: <R k="attnGrowth.rows.0.meanStats.-1.maxScore" d={0} /> and <R k="attnGrowth.rows.0.meanStats.-1.attnEntropy" d={2} />). The run does not blow up; it just learns less: <R k="attnGrowth.rows.3.mean" d={3} /> ± <R k="attnGrowth.rows.3.sd" d={3} /> against <R k="attnGrowth.rows.0.mean" d={3} /> ± <R k="attnGrowth.rows.0.sd" d={3} /> at 0.01.

**The cures.** _QK-norm_ applies a LayerNorm to each head's queries and keys before the dot product, so the scores cannot grow just because the weights do (only the norm's gain can scale them). With it, the run at 0.1 keeps its largest score at <R k="attnGrowth.rows.4.meanStats.-1.maxScore" d={0} /> and reaches <R k="attnGrowth.rows.4.mean" d={3} /> ± <R k="attnGrowth.rows.4.sd" d={3} />: the best of every run in this chapter, because the higher learning rate is now usable. _z-loss_ ([Chowdhery et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311): PaLM added $10^{-4}\log^2 Z$ to the loss; here the coefficient is $10^{-3}$) pulls the output softmax's normaliser $\log Z$ towards 0; it targets a different instability, the output logits drifting, and does nothing for attention: with it the largest score still reaches <R k="attnGrowth.rows.5.meanStats.-1.maxScore" d={0} sep /> and the loss <R k="attnGrowth.rows.5.mean" d={3} /> ± <R k="attnGrowth.rows.5.sd" d={3} />. The plainest cure is a lower learning rate, which costs speed.

**A loss spike: one bad batch.** The second experiment trains with momentum (no adaptive scaling) at a learning rate of <R k="badBatch.rows.0.lr" />, and at step <R k="badBatch.at" sep /> feeds one batch that is nothing like the book: every window the character "@" repeated. Its gradient is huge. Without protection the step throws the weights far off: the validation loss jumps from <R k="badBatch.rows.0.before.0" d={2} /> to <R k="badBatch.rows.0.after.0" d={1} /> and the run then diverges (seed 1; all three seeds do the same, results.md §8a). _Gradient clipping_ ([Pascanu et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5063)) rescales any gradient whose norm exceeds a threshold (1.0 here, and in the default run): the same bad batch now moves the loss from <R k="badBatch.rows.1.before.0" d={3} /> to <R k="badBatch.rows.1.after.0" d={3} />, and the run carries on. _Skipping_ a batch whose gradient norm is far above the recent average (here 4×) avoids even that. AdamW at its usual learning rate barely notices the batch even unclipped (<R k="badBatch.rows.3.before.0" d={3} /> → <R k="badBatch.rows.3.after.0" d={3} />): Adam divides each step by the running size of past gradients, so one large gradient cannot make one large step.

<BadBatchChart />

**What large runs report.** PaLM's largest model spiked about 20 times despite clipping; the fix was to restart from a checkpoint about 100 steps before the spike and skip the 200–500 batches around it. Its authors note the spikes were _not_ caused by bad data as such: the same batches did not spike from another checkpoint ([Chowdhery et al. 2022, §5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.02311)). [Molybog et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09871) argue that such divergence is an artifact of Adam itself: it can enter a state where its update is large yet essentially uncorrelated with the direction that lowers the loss, more often in deep models trained with large batches. So the usual toolkit is several guards at once: warmup, clipping, QK-norm or z-loss, a lower $\beta_2$ (LLaMA used 0.95; [Touvron et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13971)), monitoring the gradient norm, and checkpoints to restart from (chapter 15).

<Layer kind="maths">

Attention entropy of row $i$: $H_i = -\sum_{j \le i} p_{ij}\log p_{ij}$, between 0 (one-hot) and $\log(i+1)$ (uniform over the $i+1$ visible positions). The softmax's backward pass $\delta s_{ij} = p_{ij}(\delta p_{ij} - \sum_k \delta p_{ik} p_{ik})$ (chapter 4) is $\approx 0$ when $p_i$ is one-hot: saturated heads stop learning.

QK-norm: $\hat q = \gamma_q \odot (q - \mu_q)/\sigma_q + \beta_q$ per head (and the same for $k$), so $|\hat q^\top \hat k| \le \|\hat q\|\|\hat k\|$ is set by the norms' gains and shifts, not by $W_Q, W_K$.

z-loss: $L = L_{\text{CE}} + c\,\overline{(\log Z)^2}$ with $\log Z = \operatorname{logsumexp}(z)$; $\partial/\partial z = 2c \log Z \cdot \operatorname{softmax}(z)$ per position, added to $p - y$.

Clipping by global norm: $g \leftarrow g \cdot \min\big(1, \tfrac{c}{\|g\| + 10^{-6}}\big)$ (PyTorch's `clip_grad_norm_`). Momentum's step is $\eta$ times a running sum of gradients, so one gradient $k$ times too large moves the weights $k$ times too far; Adam's is $\eta\, \hat m/\sqrt{\hat v}$, bounded by about $\eta$ per coordinate.

```python
def z_loss_terms(logits, probs, coeff):
    """PaLM's z-loss, coeff * mean(lse^2), and its gradient 2 coeff lse p / n."""
    m = logits.max(-1, keepdims=True)
    lse = m[..., 0] + np.log(np.exp(logits - m).sum(-1))
    n = lse.size
    return coeff * float((lse * lse).mean()), (2 * coeff / n) * lse[..., None] * probs
```
